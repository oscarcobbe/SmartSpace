What we found

Over seven days, the trouble was in your broadband connection, not in your home, and it was at its worst every evening.

The internet went completely 6 times, for 47 minutes in all, while your router stayed up and answering every second of it. 5 of those 6 drops came between 18:00 and 23:00.

A video call would have stuttered or frozen in 1275 of the 10,042 minutes measured, and 86% of those trouble minutes fell in the evening. Evening download was 80 Mbps on a typical test, against 196 Mbps the rest of the day, on a 300 Mbps package.

You are welcome to send this report to your provider. Everything in it was measured on a cable at your router, so your Wi-Fi plays no part in it.

How it was measured

A small box of ours, plugged into your router with a cable and left for a week.

Every minute, day and night, the box sent 20 test signals to the internet and 20 to your router, and timed each one coming back. A signal that never comes back is a lost packet: on a video call that is a frozen picture or a dropped word.

Checking the router at the same moment tells the two kinds of fault apart. If the router answers and the internet does not, the problem is beyond your home. Once an hour the box also ran a speed test, 167 in all, and measured the delay while the line was busy.

The box was unplugged for 38 minutes on Wed 7 Oct afternoon. Those minutes are left out rather than guessed.

What to do next

Our advice, based on the week above.

  • Send this report to your provider and ask them to look at the mast your router uses, quoting the evening loss of 6.2% and the 6 drops with times.
  • Ask about moving the router to an upstairs window facing the mast, or about an outdoor antenna. Your provider can tell you which way the mast is.
  • If they cannot fix it, a fixed line (fibre where available) would be the dependable choice for work calls. We can repeat this test on the new line so you can compare.

What we are not recommending

New Wi-Fi equipment. Your router answered our box in every minute except one restart, so the house side is not what is freezing your calls. Spending on mesh or powerline now would not fix this.
